# HAL Book X Project Instructions

Book I is the supreme constitutional authority.
Book II is the authoritative architecture.
Book III defines mandatory engineering standards.
Book X defines the canonical terminology and information model shared across the HAL Canon.

Read Books I, II, and III before adopting a definition.
Book X MUST preserve the meaning and authority of the source books. It MUST NOT create a constitutional principle, redesign the architecture, weaken an engineering control, specify a component implementation, or invent an interface contract.

For every canonical term, maintain:
- preferred label and stable term ID;
- precise definition;
- scope and semantic type;
- source citations;
- relationships and constraints;
- lifecycle or state semantics where applicable;
- allowed synonyms;
- deprecated and forbidden usages;
- examples and counterexamples;
- cross-book references;
- review status.

Distinguish terms that are related but not interchangeable, especially identity, authentication, trust, authority, permission, delegation, capability, intent, action, transaction, evidence, verification, and certification.

Use one stable meaning per term. When a source uses a word in more than one legitimate sense, define qualified terms rather than collapsing the meanings.

Record ambiguity, contradiction, and proposed reconciliation. If Book X conflicts with a higher-order source, preserve the higher-order meaning and correct Book X.

Work autonomously on routine semantic, editorial, modeling, and naming decisions. Raise an Owner Review item only when resolving a definition would interpret constitutional philosophy, alter Owner authority, change a constitutional invariant, create a capability or Treaty class, accept substantial irreversible risk, resolve a major human-value conflict, or make a long-term stewardship choice that evidence cannot settle.

Maintain bidirectional traceability, a concept register, an entity-relationship register, lifecycle/state definitions, an acronym register, a deprecated-term register, a forbidden-ambiguity register, and a cross-book term index.

Do not claim completion while a material term remains unmapped, a source conflict is unresolved, or a required deliverable is absent.
